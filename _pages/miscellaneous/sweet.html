The ultimate chocolate cake

I love chocolate cakes. I try to make one for each birthday. For a really long time (and I probably mean for the last ten years) I have been failing at making them as I wanted, i.e., not too sweet, crispy at the top and slightly melting at the bottom. After years of experimenting, here's the ultimate recipe:

Ingredients

  • 200g chocolate (I take it with 70% cocoa)
  • 100g butter
  • 60g flour
  • 3 eggs
  • 10g of sugar (literally)

Steps

Preheat the oven in fan mode at 180°C.

Grease a plate (I have a 20cm-squared glass one).

Break the chocolate in tiny pieces.

Dice the butter in small cubes.

In a double boiler, melt the chocolate.

When the chocolate is melted, add small pieces of butter, one after another.

In the meantime, whip up the eggs (combined, yolk and white together) until the preparation triples in volume.

When all the butter is incorporated, add the flour to the chocolate preparation (this is key!).

Combine the two preparations by adding the chocolate mixture to the eggs. Mix very delicately until the preparation is homogenous. Avoid overmixing to prevent the eggs from collapsing.

Pour the preparation into the greased plate.

Bake for 20 minutes, or until you can slide a knife in and it comes out clean.

You're done!

Parfait à la rhubarbe et coulis à la framboise (Rhubarb Parfait with Raspberry Coulis)

I created this recipe after a bit of trial and error. I am particularly proud of it because it's possible to make it in batch and freeze it. It's a bit advanced, but if you follow the steps carefully, it will be worth it! Aside from the usual cooking utensils, you will need a cooking thermometer.

Ingredients

  • 500g rhubarb
  • 100g raspberries
  • 20cL cream (liquid)
  • 3 eggs
  • 50g water
  • 100g sugar

Steps

    In two separate pots, melt the raspberry and the rhubarb, at low temperature. Stir occasionally. When the preparation is smooth, let it cool at room temperature.

    In the meantime, in a cold bowl, whip up the cream in a chantilly. You can add a bit of sugar & vanilla. Once done, let it cool at room temperature.

    Sabayon

    With the eggs, the sugar and the water, prepare a sabayon:

    Put the water and the sugar in a small pan and heat until 118°C (this is why we need a thermometer!).

    In the meantime, whisk the eggs in a beater until the preparation doubles in volume and becomes whiter.

    Once the sugar + water has reached 118°C add slowly to the eggs while still beating. The idea is that the heated sugar will "cook" the preparation.

    Continue whipping the preparation until it cools down (you can touch the bowl to get an idea).

    When all the (i) rhubarb (ii) whipped cream and (iii) sabayon preparations are roughly the same temperature, combine together and mix delicately so that the sabayon does not fall down

    Staging:

    Put the preparation in a piping bag with your favourite 1cm nozzle.

You're done!

Italian style hot chocolate

To warm up my winters, I love to make this hot chocolate recipe, as I found it in Torino! This recipe makes a very thick hot chocolate, almost like a chocolate sauce! Really filling.

Ingredients for 1 person

  • 30g cocoa powder
  • 10g cornstarch
  • 5g sugar
  • 1 glass of milk

Steps

In a hot pot, add the cocoa powder, the cornstarch and the sugar. Mix very delicately.

When the dry preparation is homogenous, carefully add in the milk.

Turn on the heat to a high temperature. Mix continuously until the preparation thickens.

Do not bring to a boil!

Add some whipped cream if you're keen!

Opéra!

I'm particularly proud of having learned to make this classic French pastry recipe! In this page, I also want to give advice on how to logistically articulate each step to maximise time efficiency. You will need a cooking thermometre as well.

Ingredients

Biscuit Joconde

  • 4 eggs
  • 150g icing sugar (note: it's just sugar put in a blender for 5 min)
  • 150g almond powder
  • 4 egg whites
  • 30g white sugar
  • 30g flour
  • 25g butter
  • Some coffee

Crème au beurre café - Butter cream with coffee flavour

  • 2 eggs
  • 200g butter at room temperature
  • 50g water
  • 100g sugar
  • Some coffee extract (it's just syrup with coffee)

Chocolate Ganache

  • 180g chocolate
  • 180mL double cream

Chocolate topping

  • Some neutral oil
  • Some cocoa powder mixed to form a liquid

Steps

Biscuit Joconde

  1. Preheat the oven to 200°C
  2. Mix the almond powder, the icing sugar and the eggs until you obtain a "ribbon effect" (it looks like a ribbon that you unfold)
  3. Add the melted butter (not too hot!). Set aside
  4. Separately, whisk the egg whites in a meringue
  5. Add the almond powder mix to the meringue. Mix carefully!
  6. Spread the preparation in a thin layer on a baking tray. You'll need three biscuits of 20cm x 20cm roughly. Usually I do this in two batches. A clean biscuit will go at the bottom, another clean one at the top, and the slightly broken one in the middle.
  7. Cook for 10 minutes in the oven.
  8. Once done, remove from the tray, melt the chocolate, and spread it on top.

Crème au beurre café (Coffee butter cream)

This part you can do in advance. You can then put the crème au beurre in a piping bag, place it in the freezer and only use it when needed! This recipe starts with a "sabayon" which we did in the rhubarb parfait. Once the sabayon is at room temperature, we add the butter & coffee, which transforms it in a butter cream.
  1. Some time prior: Take the butter, dice it, and let it at room temperature. This needs to be room temperature for when you start the recipe.
  2. Put the water and the sugar in a small pan and heat until 118°C (this is why we need a thermometer!).
  3. In the meantime, whisk the eggs in a beater until the preparation doubles in volume and becomes whiter.
  4. Once the sugar + water has reached 118°C add slowly to the eggs while still beating. The idea is that the heated sugar will "cook" the preparation.
  5. Continue whipping the preparation until it cools down (you can touch the bowl to get an idea).
  6. You need to wait... A certain amount of time until the preparation is room temperature and the butter as well. And this will be super important. The sabayon cannot be warmer than the butter. If anything it needs to be cooler. When I'm in a rush, I put the preparation over a bowl of cold water and ice...
  7. When everything is room temperature, this is when you can start adding the butter. Whisk the preparation again, and add little by little dices of butter. You'll know from the first one whether you are on your way to success or failure.
  8. Once you have added all the butter, add the coffee extract.
  9. Store in a piping bag if you'll freeze it. If not, cover the preparation and leave in the fridge before staging

Ganache au chocolat

This is the easiest part! Super quick and you can do it on the day.
  • Melt the cream in a saucepan at medium temperature.
  • When you can see it form bubbles, add the chocolate in three separate increments.
  • The consistency should be like a thick sauce. Leave in the fridge, and upon using take out and mix such that it has the right texture.

Staging

Align as follows:

  1. One layer of biscuit, which you have covered in chocolate at the bottom
  2. One layer of butter cream
  3. One layer of biscuit
  4. One layer of chocolate ganache
  5. One layer of biscuit
  6. One layer of coffee cream
  7. Then finish with a layer of the chocolate topping: pour some chocolate liquid in the middle and rotate the tin such that it is harmoniously covering the butter cream.

You're done!
