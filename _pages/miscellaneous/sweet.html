Cooking Recipes

The ultimate chocolate cake

I love chocolate cakes. I try to make one for each birthday. For a really long time (and I probably mean for the last ten years) I have been failing at making them as I wanted, i.e., not too sweet, crispy at the top and slightly melting at the bottom. After years of experimenting, here's the ultimate recipe:

Ingredients

  • 200g chocolate (I take it with 70% cocoa)
  • 100g butter
  • 60g flour
  • 3 eggs
  • 10g of sugar (literally)

Steps

Preheat the oven in fan mode at 180°C.

Grease a plate (I have a 20cm-squared glass one).

Break the chocolate in tiny pieces.

Dice the butter in small cubes.

In a double boiler, melt the chocolate.

When the chocolate is melted, add small pieces of butter, one after another.

In the meantime, whip up the eggs (combined, yolk and white together) until the preparation triples in volume.

When all the butter is incorporated, add the flour to the chocolate preparation (this is key!).

Combine the two preparations by adding the chocolate mixture to the eggs. Mix very delicately until the preparation is homogenous. Avoid overmixing to prevent the eggs from collapsing.

Pour the preparation into the greased plate.

Bake for 20 minutes, or until you can slide a knife in and it comes out clean.

You're done!

Italian style hot chocolate

To warm up my winters, I love to make this hot chocolate recipe, as I found it in Torino! This recipe makes a very thick hot chocolate, almost like a chocolate sauce! Really filling.

Ingredients for 1 person

  • 30g cocoa powder
  • 10g cornstarch
  • 5g sugar
  • 1 glass of milk

Steps

In a hot pot, add the cocoa powder, the cornstarch and the sugar. Mix very delicately.

When the dry preparation is homogenous, carefully add in the milk.

Turn on the heat to a high temperature. Mix continuously until the preparation thickens.

Do not bring to a boil!

Add some whipped cream if you're keen!

Parfait à la rhubarbe et coulis à la framboise (Rhubarb Parfait with Raspberry Coulis)

I created this recipe after a bit of trial and error. I am particularly proud of it because it's possible to make it in batch and freeze it. It's a bit advanced, but if you follow the steps carefully, it will be worth it! Aside from the usual cooking utensils, you will need a cooking thermometer.

Ingredients

  • 500g rhubarb
  • 100g raspberries
  • 20cL cream (liquid)
  • 3 eggs
  • 50g water
  • 100g sugar

Steps

    In two separate pots, melt the raspberry and the rhubarb, at low temperature. Stir occasionally. When the preparation is smooth, let it cool at room temperature.

    In the meantime, in a cold bowl, whip up the cream in a chantilly. You can add a bit of sugar & vanilla. Once done, let it cool at room temperature.

    Sabayon

    With the eggs, the sugar and the water, prepare a sabayon:

    Put the water and the sugar in a small pan and heat until 118°C (this is why we need a thermometer!).

    In the meantime, whisk the eggs in a beater until the preparation doubles in volume and becomes whiter.

    Once the sugar + water has reached 118°C add slowly to the eggs while still beating. The idea is that the heated sugar will "cook" the preparation.

    Continue whipping the preparation until it cools down (you can touch the bowl to get an idea).

    When all the (i) rhubarb (ii) whipped cream and (iii) sabayon preparations are roughly the same temperature, combine together and mix delicately so that the sabayon does not fall down

    Staging:

    Put the preparation in a piping bag with your favourite 1cm nozzle.

You're done!

Pâte à Choux (Choux Buns)

A classic, and I'm not inventing something here, but since it is referenced in recipes below, then it's good to have it written down somewhere.

Ingredients (I think for 20 small choux)

  • 100g of butter
  • 60mL of water
  • 65mL of milk (you can switch the proportions of milk and water, as long as the total adds to 125mL)
  • a pinch of salt
  • a pinch of sugar
  • 4 eggs

Steps

  • Pre-heat the oven at 180°C.
  • Melt the butter with the milk, the water, the salt and sugar (with Kenbot, my kitchen aid, I heat at 100°C for 3 min with the soft beater).
  • Once melted, bring briefly to a boil.
  • Then turn off the heat, remove the pan from the hob
  • Mix really well to "dry" the preparation. The more you do that, the more you'll need to add eggs later. A good rule of thumb is that there should be a very thin layer forming at the bottom of the pot.
  • Add the flour and mix very well (Kenbot: speed 2).
  • In the meantime (if the robot works for you) or afterwards, mix the eggs together
  • Add the eggs to the preparation like so: first a half of the mixture, then mix, once combined, add a half of the mixture, once combined repeat...
  • It is quite hard to explain what the preparation should look like, but there are two tests (technically three), very well explained in this video by Mélanie Mayné
    1. The rooster: If you take a spatula, dip into the mixture and lift the spatula, there should be a shape in the form of the neck of a rooster.
    2. The river: If you dip the spatula in a line across the mixture, then it should form a hole that quickly closes (like a river)
  • Once done, put in a piping bag with the nozzle of your choosing.
  • On a baking tray, lay down the choux in the desired size. I think to start with it's better to try with small choux (like 4cm diameter).
  • To give them the proper shape, you can use a paint brush dipped in either some water or some of the egg mixture above.
  • Cook in the oven at 180°C for 30 minutes. You cannot open the door of the oven while this takes places, otherwise your choux will fall down! For the first attempt, it's probably best to lean more on the side of "over-cooking" rather than the reverse.

Praliné

I think this is one of the essentials any French pastry baker needs to have in her fridge! It's really easy, and you can even use it as a spread. You can do this with hazelnut only!

Ingredients

  • 100g of sugar (honestly I've been trying to reduce that)
  • 67mL of water
  • 100g of almonds
  • 100g of hazelnuts

Steps

  • Mix the water & sugar, bring to a boil
  • Then add the mix of almonds and hazelnuts
  • Stir constantly! And turn off the heat
  • The mixture should become a bit caramelised and brown, but don't overdo it.
  • Lay on a baking sheet.
  • Once it is cool, put in a blender, mix. The blender needs to be strong! You can add a little bit of neutral oil to launch it.
  • The mixture should start becoming oily.
  • You're done! Store in the fridge.

My version of the Paris-Brest

Paris-Brest is a chou bun (usually round-shaped like a donut) which is filled with a crème mousseline with hazelnut flavour. I move a little bit away from this recipe. I use a butter cream instead of a crème mousseline. The purists in the room may scream, and I do apologise. But I think this recipe is a nice link with the Rhubarb Parfait (above) and the Opéra (below), and also it's freezable. So please forgive my transgression. Note that you'll need the essential "praliné" recipe, referenced above, which you should always have in your fridge!

Ingredients (I think for 20 choux)

Pâte à choux

  • The ingredients of the pâte à choux (referenced above) for 20 choux

Crème au beurre praliné (Butter cream with hazelnut flavour)

  • Some praliné (see above)
  • 2 eggs
  • 100g of sugar
  • 50g of water
  • 200g of butter at room temperature. In terms of logistics, you may want to take it out during the time where you prepare the pâte à choux.

Steps

Pâte à choux

  • Prepare the pâte à choux like above, and make the 20 choux of 4cm diameter.

Butter cream

The butter cream is essentially a sabayon in which we add butter at room temperature. Super delicate and tricky!
  1. Some time prior: Take the butter, dice it, and let it at room temperature. This needs to be room temperature for when you start the recipe.
  2. Put the water and the sugar in a small pan and heat until 118°C (this is why we need a thermometer!).
  3. In the meantime, whisk the eggs in a beater until the preparation doubles in volume and becomes whiter.
  4. Once the sugar + water has reached 118°C add slowly to the eggs while still beating. The idea is that the heated sugar will "cook" the preparation.
  5. Continue whipping the preparation until it cools down (you can touch the bowl to get an idea).
  6. You need to wait... A certain amount of time until the preparation is room temperature and the butter as well. And this will be super important. The sabayon cannot be warmer than the butter. If anything it needs to be cooler. When I'm in a rush, I put the preparation over a bowl of cold water and ice...
  7. When everything is room temperature, this is when you can start adding the butter. Whisk the preparation again, and add little by little dices of butter. You'll know from the first one whether you are on your way to success or failure.
  8. Once you have added all the butter, add the praliné to taste.
  9. Store in a piping bag with a 2cm nozzle.

Staging

  • Take one chou, and poke a hole in the bottom with a sharp knife that you twist. The hole should thus be 2cm diameter. Alternatively, you can slice the top (but then you'll see it).
  • Then insert the crème au beurre, to fill half of it.
  • Add a spoon of praliné in the middle.
  • Fill to the top.

You're done!

Opéra!

I'm particularly proud of having learned to make this classic French pastry recipe! In this page, I also want to give advice on how to logistically articulate each step to maximise time efficiency. You will need a cooking thermometre as well. I won't lie, this recipe is hard. To avoid disappointment, you may want to try doing the Rhubarb Parfait first (to learn how to make a sabayon) and then maybe a modified version of the Paris-Brest (to learn how to make the butter cream).

Ingredients

Biscuit Joconde

  • 4 eggs
  • 150g icing sugar (note: it's just sugar put in a blender for 5 min)
  • 150g almond powder
  • 4 egg whites
  • 30g white sugar
  • 30g flour
  • 25g butter
  • Some chocolate
  • Some coffee

Crème au beurre café - Butter cream with coffee flavour

  • 2 eggs
  • 200g butter at room temperature
  • 50g water
  • 100g sugar
  • Some coffee extract (it's just syrup with coffee)

Chocolate Ganache

  • 180g chocolate
  • 180mL double cream

Chocolate topping

  • Some neutral oil
  • Some cocoa powder mixed to form a liquid
Note on the steps: The recipe is quite lengthy. To optimise, this is what I would suggest: make the coffee extract, the coffee butter cream in advance, maybe the ganache (although when it's really cold it's hard to work it easily).

Steps

Biscuit Joconde

  1. Preheat the oven to 200°C
  2. Mix the almond powder, the icing sugar and the eggs until the preparation doubles in volume and gets whiter.
  3. Add the melted butter (not too hot!).
  4. Add the flour. Set aside.
  5. Separately, whisk the egg whites with the 30g sugar in a meringue.
  6. Add the almond powder mix to the meringue. Mix carefully!
  7. Spread the preparation in a thin layer on a baking tray but not too transparent. You'll need three biscuits of 20cm x 20cm roughly. Usually I do this in two batches. A clean biscuit will go at the bottom, another clean one at the top, and the two broken halfs in the middle.
  8. Cook for 10 minutes in the oven.
  9. Once done, remove from the tray, melt the chocolate, and spread it on top.

Crème au beurre café (Coffee butter cream)

This part you can do in advance. You can then put the crème au beurre in a piping bag, place it in the freezer and only use it when needed! This recipe starts with a "sabayon" which we did in the rhubarb parfait. Once the sabayon is at room temperature, we add the butter & coffee, which transforms it in a butter cream.
  1. Some time prior: Take the butter, dice it, and let it at room temperature. This needs to be room temperature for when you start the recipe.
  2. Put the water and the sugar in a small pan and heat until 118°C (this is why we need a thermometer!).
  3. In the meantime, whisk the eggs in a beater until the preparation doubles in volume and becomes whiter.
  4. Once the sugar + water has reached 118°C add slowly to the eggs while still beating. The idea is that the heated sugar will "cook" the preparation.
  5. Continue whipping the preparation until it cools down (you can touch the bowl to get an idea).
  6. You need to wait... A certain amount of time until the preparation is room temperature and the butter as well. And this will be super important. The sabayon cannot be warmer than the butter. If anything it needs to be cooler. When I'm in a rush, I put the preparation over a bowl of cold water and ice...
  7. When everything is room temperature, this is when you can start adding the butter. Whisk the preparation again, and add little by little dices of butter. You'll know from the first one whether you are on your way to success or failure.
  8. Once you have added all the butter, add the coffee extract.
  9. Store in a piping bag if you'll freeze it. If not, cover the preparation and leave in the fridge before staging

Ganache au chocolat

This is the easiest part! Super quick and you can do it on the day.
  1. Melt the cream in a saucepan at medium temperature.
  2. When you can see it form bubbles, add the chocolate in three separate increments.
  3. The consistency should be like a thick sauce. Leave in the fridge, and upon using take out and mix such that it has the right texture.

Staging

Align as follows:

  1. One layer of biscuit, which you have covered in chocolate at the bottom (so the chocolate is against the tray).
  2. With a brush, cover the "clean" part of the biscuit with coffee
  3. One layer of butter cream
  4. One layer of biscuit
  5. One layer of chocolate ganache
  6. One layer of biscuit
  7. One layer of coffee cream
  8. Then finish with a layer of the chocolate topping: pour some chocolate liquid in the middle and rotate the tin such that it is harmoniously covering the butter cream.

You're done!
